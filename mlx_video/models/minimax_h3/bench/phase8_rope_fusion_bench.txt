Phase 8-4 microbenchmark: is a custom fused RMSNorm+RoPE Metal kernel worth writing?

Setup
-----
  H3Attention at hidden=5376, heads=56, head_dim=128, seq=2265 (Config A),
  bf16 activations. Ran on the same Mac Studio as Phase 8-3.

Component timings (ms/call, mean of 200 iters, mx.eval after each)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  full H3Attention (bf16)                : 30.20 ms
  mx.fast.rms_norm (already-fused kernel):  0.628 ms
  apply_split_half_rope                  :  0.661 ms
  eager  (rms_norm + rope)               :  1.527 ms
  mx.compile(rms_norm + rope)            :  1.170 ms

Compile fusion speedup on the norm+rope fragment: 1.30x
Fraction of one attention forward spent in norm+rope (q + k): 8.5%
Projected end-to-end DiT-step speedup if the fragment were 1.30x faster: 1.020x

Decision
--------
Skip. The dominant cost is mx.fast.scaled_dot_product_attention (~90% of the
attention forward), and mx.fast.rms_norm is already a fused Metal kernel.
Even a perfect custom fused kernel over the norm+rope fragment would yield
~2% end-to-end DiT-step speedup, well under the 1.2x threshold from the
Phase 8 plan.

Higher-yield future optimizations
---------------------------------
- Attention: at seq_len 6.4k -> 39k the step time grew 19x for a 6.1x token
  increase (near-quadratic). A custom tiled + causal-mask-free Metal kernel
  for H3's specific attention shape (packed sequence with mixed video/audio/
  text tokens, no chunking) could realistically halve the attention time at
  large seq. This is Phase 9-tier work.
- KV cache for the ref-conditioning blocks (they don't change across
  denoising steps). Would save one full attention-vs-refs pass per step.
- Pipeline scaling: chunked attention along the temporal axis, similar to
  the video-VAE tiling already deferred from Phase 2.
